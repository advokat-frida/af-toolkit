## Timing and scope controls

These are enforcement procedures, not permission to implement the law late. Keep them separate from consumer-response/appeal clocks, initial commencement dates, phased risk-assessment/ADMT deadlines and institution-specific compliance dates.

- California's [§1798.150(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.150.) has a separate 30-day consumer-notice rule for specified breach claims seeking statutory damages. It does not reinstate the former general regulatory cure right. No wider private-action conclusion is included.
- Colorado's remaining protection is limited to the three named minors provisions. A blanket “no cure in Colorado” or “Colorado gives 60 days” would both be misleading.
- Florida's 45-day cure is conditional on a Department grant. It is not the consumer-request 45-day period and does not inherit that period's possible extension.
- Maryland's cure applies by the alleged violation's date, **through April 1, 2027 inclusive**. Do not substitute a filing-date or notice-date sunset. Its implementation and processing-date provisions are separate.
- Minnesota's institutional implementation exception in the effective-date note is not a revived cure period.
