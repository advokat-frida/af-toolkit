# PWC enforcement/cure: second ten states

Current checkpoint: **2026-09-22 UTC**. Scope: the current comprehensive privacy law's cure opportunity and enforcement actor for MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, RI, TN, TX, UT and VA. This is a local draft legal-content candidate. Practitioner and release gates remain untouched.

A cure period is a pre-enforcement process triggered by the specified regulator's notice. It is not permission to wait that many days before implementing the law, nor a consumer-request response/appeal deadline. Where the statute grants discretion or no current opportunity, receipt of an allegation does not itself create a cure entitlement. Historic notices may require case-specific analysis; this packet addresses current entitlement rather than adjudicating an old matter.

## Montana

- **Primary authority:** [MCA §30-14-2817 (2025 code, as amended by 2025 ch. 567 §8)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0300/chapter_0140/part_0280/section_0170/0300-0140-0280-0170.html). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Montana Attorney General; the current section grants exclusive enforcement authority and permits civil investigative demands on reasonable cause.
- **Notice trigger:** Current §30-14-2817 contains no mandatory pre-enforcement cure notice or cure period.
- **Current entitlement:** No current cure entitlement in this provision.
- **Timing / sunset:** The current 2025-amended section omits the former cure provision. No former 60-day cure should be presented as current on September 22, 2026; the exact amendment-commencement date is not separately stated in the cited code page.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Route an allegation or investigative demand promptly to the legal owner and preserve the notice, allegations and response deadline. Do not assume a fresh 60-day implementation period or delay correction while waiting for a cure notice.

Source record: `pwc-us-mt-duties-enforcement`.

## Nebraska

- **Primary authority:** [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§87-1119, 87-1122, 87-1124](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/laws-index/chap87-full.html). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Nebraska Attorney General; §87-1119 grants exclusive authority.
- **Notice trigger:** Before an action under §87-1124, the Attorney General must give the controller or processor written notice identifying the specific allegedly violated provisions, at least thirty days before the action.
- **Current entitlement:** Mandatory notice and conditional cure bar.
- **Timing / sunset:** No sunset appears in the current §§87-1119/1122/1124.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Record the Attorney General's written notice, the identified provisions and the thirty-day period before the proposed action. Within that period, cure the identified violation and provide the Attorney General a written cure statement with supportive documentation, plus an express written commitment not to commit such a violation after cure. Keep this enforcement-cure period separate from consumer-request deadlines and the law's implementation date.

Source record: `pwc-us-ne-duties-enforcement`.

## New Hampshire

- **Primary authority:** [RSA 507-H:11(I)–(III), (V)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/507-H/507-H-11.htm). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** New Hampshire Attorney General; exclusive authority under paragraph I and enforcement through the stated RSA 358-A route.
- **Notice trigger:** Since January 1, 2026, before an action the Attorney General may issue a violation notice if it determines cure is possible. Failure to cure within sixty days after receipt permits an action.
- **Current entitlement:** Discretionary opportunity, 60 days if granted.
- **Timing / sunset:** Mandatory, cure-possible notice applied January 1 through December 31, 2025. Beginning January 1, 2026, the opportunity is discretionary.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Identify whether the Attorney General has issued a cure notice; if so, record receipt and the sixty-day cure deadline. Address the alleged violation and document the factors relevant to the Attorney General's discretion: violation count, entity size/complexity, processing, public injury, safety, and human or technical error. Do not apply the mandatory 2025 notice rule or confuse this period with consumer-response timing.

Source record: `pwc-us-nh-duties-enforcement`.

## New Jersey

- **Primary authority:** [N.J.S.A. §§56:8-166.17(b), 56:8-166.19; P.L. 2023, c. 266 §§14,16–17](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2022/PL23/266_.HTM). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Office of the New Jersey Attorney General has sole and exclusive enforcement authority; the temporary cure notice was issued by the Division of Consumer Affairs.
- **Notice trigger:** While operative, the Division of Consumer Affairs had to issue notice to the controller before an administrative/court enforcement action if cure was deemed possible, allowing thirty days after receipt.
- **Current entitlement:** Temporary mandatory opportunity expired.
- **Timing / sunset:** The statutory cutoff is the first day of the eighteenth month next following the January 15, 2025 effective date: July 1, 2026. The thirty-day opportunity is no longer guaranteed at the September 22, 2026 checkpoint.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Route enforcement correspondence to the legal owner without assuming the former thirty-day cure protection. If a matter involves a notice issued during the temporary window, preserve its receipt date and terms for counsel to assess; a historical notice is not a new implementation grace period. Keep the statutory July 1, 2026 cutoff distinct from the Act's January 15, 2025 effective date.

Source record: `pwc-us-nj-duties-enforcement`.

## Oregon

- **Primary authority:** [ORS 646A.589(4), (7); 2025 Or. Laws ch. 417 §§4–6 (note following §646A.589)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646a.html). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Oregon Attorney General; exclusive authority under §646A.589(7).
- **Notice trigger:** The temporary rule required notice before a §646A.589(4) action when the Attorney General determined the controller could cure, with thirty days after receipt.
- **Current entitlement:** General and temporary broadcaster opportunities expired.
- **Timing / sunset:** From January 1, 2026 the temporary rule applied only to the defined qualifying noncommercial educational broadcasters. Chapter 417 §6 repealed that remaining rule on July 1, 2026. At September 22, 2026 neither the general nor that broadcaster opportunity remains.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Treat current enforcement correspondence as requiring prompt legal review; do not assume a statutory thirty-day cure before an action. For a historical matter, distinguish the former general rule from the January-to-June 2026 exception for the precisely defined noncommercial educational broadcasters. Keep cure history separate from current substantive compliance and consumer-request deadlines.

Source record: `pwc-us-or-duties-enforcement`.

## Rhode Island

- **Primary authority:** [R.I. Gen. Laws §6-48.1-8(a)–(b)](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/6-48.1-8.htm). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Rhode Island Attorney General; sole enforcement authority for this chapter.
- **Notice trigger:** Section 6-48.1-8 assigns enforcement and identifies the commercial-law/deceptive-trade-practice route; it does not prescribe a mandatory cure notice or cure period.
- **Current entitlement:** No cure entitlement supplied by this chapter.
- **Timing / sunset:** No statutory cure window or sunset is stated in this comprehensive privacy chapter, effective January 1, 2026.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Route an alleged violation or enforcement notice to the legal owner promptly instead of applying another state's thirty- or sixty-day cure rule. Assess the procedure actually invoked under §6-48.1-8 and the referenced commercial-law provisions; this entry does not determine separate procedural rights under another law. Keep the January 1, 2026 implementation date separate from any deadline stated in actual enforcement correspondence.

Source record: `pwc-us-ri-duties-enforcement`.

## Tennessee

- **Primary authority:** [Tenn. Code Ann. §47-18-3313(a)–(c)](https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Archives/Joint/publications/TNCodeBills/2025/TNCodeBill_Volume1_2025.pdf). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Tennessee Attorney General and Reporter; exclusive enforcement authority.
- **Notice trigger:** Before an action under this part, the Attorney General and Reporter must provide the controller or processor sixty days' written notice identifying the specific allegedly violated provisions.
- **Current entitlement:** Mandatory notice and conditional cure bar.
- **Timing / sunset:** The provision is effective July 1, 2025 and states no cure sunset.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Record the written notice, specific allegations and sixty-day cure period. Within the period, cure the noticed violation and provide an express written statement that the alleged violations are cured and no such further violations will occur. Track the corrective controls: continued violations or breach of that statement can permit an action. This cure period is separate from implementation and consumer-response deadlines.

Source record: `pwc-us-tn-duties-enforcement`.

## Texas

- **Primary authority:** [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§541.151, 541.154, 541.155(a)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.541.htm). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Texas Attorney General; exclusive enforcement authority under §541.151.
- **Notice trigger:** Before a §541.155 action, the Attorney General must notify the person in writing at least thirty days beforehand, identifying the specific allegedly violated provisions.
- **Current entitlement:** Mandatory notice and conditional cure bar.
- **Timing / sunset:** Effective July 1, 2024; no cure sunset in these current provisions.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Record the Attorney General's written notice, alleged provisions and thirty-day period; cure the identified violation within that period. Provide a written statement confirming cure; notification to the affected consumer if that consumer's contact information was made available to the person; supportive documentation showing cure; and necessary internal-policy changes to prevent such further violations. Preserve proof of each condition. The cure process does not postpone ordinary compliance or consumer-response duties.

Source record: `pwc-us-tx-duties-enforcement`.

## Utah

- **Primary authority:** [Utah Code §§13-61-401, 13-61-402(1)–(3)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title 13/Chapter 61/C13-61_2022050420231231.pdf). Retrieved September 22, 2026.
- **Enforcement actor:** Utah Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority upon referral from the Division of Consumer Protection; the Division receives/investigates complaints and refers on the statutory substantial-evidence standard.
- **Notice trigger:** Upon the Division's referral, and at least thirty days before initiating an action, the Attorney General must provide the controller or processor written notice identifying each alleged provision and explaining the basis for each allegation.
- **Current entitlement:** Mandatory notice and conditional cure bar.
- **Timing / sunset:** No cure sunset is stated in current §13-61-402. The future January 2027 vehicle provisions are not this cure deadline.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Preserve the Attorney General's notice and its explanation of each allegation; record when it was received. Cure within thirty days after the day of receipt and provide an express written statement that the violation is cured and no further occurrence of the cured violation will happen. Keep the Division referral, notice and proof of correction together. Continued violations after cure can permit enforcement; consumer-response and future implementation dates remain separate.

Source record: `pwc-us-ut-duties-enforcement`.

## Virginia

- **Enforcement actor:** Virginia Attorney General; exclusive enforcement authority for this chapter.
- **Notice trigger:** Before any action under this chapter, the Attorney General must give the controller or processor thirty days' written notice identifying the specific provisions alleged to have been or to be violated.
- **Current entitlement:** Mandatory notice and conditional cure bar.
- **Timing / sunset:** The current provision states no cure sunset.
- **Conditions and next steps:** Record the Attorney General's written notice, the specific allegedly violated provisions and the thirty-day period. Within the period, cure the noticed violation and provide an express written statement that the alleged violations are cured and no further violations will occur. Maintain the corrective controls: a continued violation or breach of the statement can permit enforcement. This is an enforcement-cure period, not an implementation extension or consumer-response deadline.

## Important source-reading distinctions

- **Montana:** the current §30-14-2817 text, amended by 2025 chapter 567, contains no former 60-day cure language. This packet therefore records no current entitlement and does not invent an exact commencement date absent from that code page.
- **Nebraska:** the relevant comprehensive-law cure is §87-1122, not the separate 2026 Agricultural Data Privacy Act's 45-day civil-penalty rule in §87-1409. Nebraska requires supportive documentation and two written assurances; Texas's separate consumer-notice condition must not be imported.
- **New Hampshire:** the statute expressly switches from shall in 2025 to may thereafter. Sixty days remains the period after a granted notice, not an automatic opportunity.
- **New Jersey:** the enacted cutoff is the first day of the eighteenth month next following the effective date, not a rolling eighteen months from January15. The Act became effective January 15, 2025 (365 days after January 16, 2024), making the month-based cutoff July 1, 2026. The period is expired under either common shorthand by this checkpoint, but the packet preserves the exact statutory calculation.
- **Oregon:** the official code includes a 2025 chapter 417 note. Section 5 narrowed cure to qualifying noncommercial educational broadcasters from January 1, 2026 and §6 repealed that remainder July 1, 2026. A generic statement that all cure simply continued for thirty days in 2026 would be wrong; so would ignoring the narrow historical extension.
- **Texas:** cure requires the written statement addressing consumer notice when contact information is available, documentation, and necessary policy changes. Correction alone is insufficient.
- **Utah:** distinguish the complaint/investigation/referral role of the Division from the Attorney General's enforcement authority. The cure clock is thirty days after the day notice is received, with the statutory written statement. January 2027 vehicle implementation dates are unrelated.
